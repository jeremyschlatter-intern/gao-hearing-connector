About This Tool

What it does: This tool connects GAO (Government Accountability Office) reports to upcoming Congressional committee hearings, so staff can quickly find relevant research and background material.

Data sources: Hearing schedules come from the Congress.gov API (committee-meeting endpoint for the 119th Congress, both chambers). GAO reports are pulled from 25 topic-specific RSS feeds on gao.gov, covering all major policy areas.

Coverage: Hearings include both House and Senate committee meetings, hearings, and markups. GAO reports typically include the ~30 most recent items per topic category, yielding ~500-600 unique reports. Reports go back several years depending on the topic.

How matching works: Each hearing and report is converted to a text representation including titles, committee names, witness organizations, and report summaries. A TF-IDF algorithm with domain-specific synonym expansion (60+ policy term groups covering health, defense, trade, energy, etc.) computes similarity scores. Scores are boosted when a report's topic area aligns with the committee's jurisdiction (e.g., Financial Markets reports get a boost for Financial Services Committee hearings). High match = strong keyword overlap (score ≥ 0.15). Medium match = moderate overlap (score 0.08–0.15). Only matches above 0.08 are shown to reduce noise. The "Matched on" terms show the specific words driving each match.

Limitations: Matching is keyword-based, not semantic. Two documents about the same topic using different terminology may not match well. The tool indexes only recent GAO reports from RSS feeds, not the complete GAO archive. Some niche topics may have limited GAO coverage.

Updates: Data refreshes automatically every morning at 6:00 AM ET via GitHub Actions.
